How to use the savings calculator

  1. Enter the amount you plan to save each month in the first field.
  2. Use the sliders to choose how long you intend to save — in years and additional months.
  3. Enter your expected annual interest rate, or drag the slider.
  4. Select your UK income tax band to apply the relevant savings interest deduction.
  5. Press Calculate to see your estimated total.

How the calculation works

The calculator uses the standard future value of an annuity formula for regular monthly contributions:

FV = P × ((1 + r)n − 1) ÷ r

Where P is your monthly contribution, r is your annual interest rate divided by 12 (the monthly rate), and n is the total number of months. Contributions are assumed to be made at the end of each month (standard ordinary annuity), and interest compounds monthly on the accumulated balance.

At 0% interest, the result is simply your monthly contribution multiplied by the number of months.

If you select a tax band, the deduction is applied to the total interest earned after allowing for the relevant UK Personal Savings Allowance (£1,000 for basic rate taxpayers; £500 for higher rate; none for additional rate). The income thresholds and allowances are based on the 2024–25 UK tax year and may no longer be current.

Things to bear in mind

  • The result is an estimate based on fixed inputs. Real savings returns will vary.
  • Interest rates on savings accounts can change at any time. The calculation assumes a constant rate throughout.
  • This calculator compounds interest monthly. Some savings products compound daily, quarterly or annually, which may produce slightly different results.
  • Inflation is not accounted for. The figure shown is in nominal terms — its purchasing power in the future may be lower.
  • Account fees, charges and individual deposit limits are not included.
  • The UK tax thresholds and Personal Savings Allowances used here are based on the 2024–25 tax year and may no longer be current.

Frequently asked questions

How is compound interest calculated?

Each month, interest is calculated on the running balance — your contributions plus interest already earned. The monthly rate is your annual rate divided by 12. Contributions are assumed to be made at the end of each month (standard ordinary annuity). This means your money grows a little faster each month as the interest base increases.

Does the calculator include tax on savings interest?

Yes. Select your UK income tax band and the calculator will apply the corresponding Personal Savings Allowance before deducting tax from the interest earned: £1,000 allowance for basic rate taxpayers (20% tax above that), £500 for higher rate taxpayers (40% above that), and no allowance for additional rate taxpayers (45% on all interest). The tax thresholds and allowances used are based on the 2024–25 UK tax year and may no longer be current.

Does the calculator account for inflation?

No. The figure shown is a nominal value in today's pounds. Inflation is not factored in, so the real purchasing power of the final amount may be lower than it appears.

Can I use a 0% interest rate?

Yes. At 0%, the result is simply your monthly contribution multiplied by the total number of months — no interest accrues and no tax is deducted.

Is the result guaranteed?

No. The result is an estimate based on a fixed interest rate and consistent monthly contributions. Actual returns depend on your specific savings product, any interest rate changes, and whether you maintain your planned contribution throughout the period.
